\section{Baseline result}

\textbf{Recogniser.} LBP$^{u2}_{16,2}$, $30\times37$ windows ($3\times3$ grid on the
$92\times112$ ORL images), $\chi^2$ distance, nearest neighbour.
\textbf{Split.} Images 1--5 of each of the 40 subjects form the gallery (200), images 6--10
the probes (200). Only the probes are changed:
\emph{bright} $0.5I+20$;
\emph{light $s$} $I + 255\,s\,(x-0.5)$ with $x\in[0,1]$ the column position (left darker, right brighter);
\emph{rot $\theta$} bilinear rotation about the centre with black corners.
All changed images are rounded and clipped to $[0,255]$.
We reconstructed these definitions from the workshop description; they reproduce the
quoted baseline numbers exactly (0.705 and 0.630).

\noindent LBP keeps only the sign of $g_p-g_c$, so a monotonic change applied to the whole
face does nothing (\emph{bright}: 0.975). Two things break it: (i) a brightness ramp adds
$c\,\Delta x_p$ to every neighbour difference and flips bits in smooth regions (cheeks,
forehead); (ii) the window grid is fixed in the image, so a rotation moves eyes, nose and
mouth into other windows.

\section{Our improvement and result}

The LBP descriptor, windows and $\chi^2$ are unchanged; we add two steps around them.

\textbf{(1) Brightness-plane subtraction} (Sung \& Poggio, 1998). Before LBP, every image
is replaced by $I'(x,y) = I(x,y) - (ax+by+c)$, where
$(a,b,c)=\arg\min \sum_{(x,y)\in\Omega}\big(I(x,y)-ax-by-c\big)^2$ and
$\Omega=\{0<I<255\}$ leaves clipped pixels out of the fit. A linear ramp is removed exactly.

\textbf{(2) Rotation search.} Every gallery image $g$ is also stored rotated by
$\Theta=\{-24^\circ,-18^\circ,\dots,24^\circ\}$ (9 copies, edge pixels repeated instead of
black corners). A probe $p$ is matched by
$d(p,g)=\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\chi^2\big(H(p'),\,H(R_\theta g')\big)$.
The test angles $5^\circ, 10^\circ, 20^\circ$ are deliberately not in $\Theta$.

\noindent\textbf{Normal photos are not hurt.} With the paper's protocol (100 random 5+5 splits,
unchanged images), the baseline gives $0.9826 \pm 0.0096$ (paper: $0.98 \pm 0.012$) and ours
gives $0.9831 \pm 0.0109$.
\textbf{Observations.} Plane subtraction alone lowers rot $20^\circ$ to 0.565; combined with
the rotation search the drop disappears. In preliminary checks, global histogram
equalisation had no effect (LBP is already invariant to monotonic maps), CLAHE lowered
\emph{normal} to 0.930, and DoG / Tan--Triggs filtering fixed lighting but lowered rot
$20^\circ$ to 0.43 or below. Cost: the gallery is $9\times$ larger, so matching is about
$9\times$ slower. A non-linear spotlight is only partly removed by a plane (0.830 $\to$ 0.900).
